Chapter 1

🧱 Cloud Service Models

FoodFast needs software, servers, storage, databases, networking and operating systems. The question is: what do you want to manage yourself?

🧑‍💻 SaaS

Use ready-made software. Provider manages almost everything.

🛠️ PaaS

Use a managed platform to build and deploy your own application.

🖥️ IaaS

Rent servers, storage and networking and manage more yourself.

SaaS — Just Use the Software

Restaurant analogy: You order food and simply eat. You do not manage the kitchen, gas, chef or utensils.
You manage: Very little

Provider manages:
Application
Updates
Backup
Support
Maintenance
Underlying environment

Examples from the lecture: Gmail, Dropbox, Prime and Netflix.

PaaS — Build on a Managed Platform

Kitchen analogy: Someone gives you a ready kitchen and tools. You bring your recipe and build your own dish.

IaaS — Rent the Infrastructure

Empty-flat analogy: You get the building and basic connections, but you decide how to set up the inside.
ModelEasy MeaningControl
SaaSUse complete softwareLeast
PaaSBuild on a managed platformMedium
IaaSRent infrastructureMore
Memory trick: I build from IaaS, develop on PaaS, and simply use SaaS.
Chapter 2

🌍 Cloud Deployment Models

Now FoodFast asks: who should be allowed to use my cloud?

🔒 Private

Used by one organization.

🌐 Public

Shared cloud for multiple customers.

👥 Community

Shared by organizations with common needs.

🔀 Hybrid

Combination of private and public cloud.

Private Cloud

Home analogy: Your house is for your family only. Likewise, a private cloud is meant for one organization.

Public Cloud

Hotel analogy: Many customers can book rooms in the same hotel. The provider runs the shared infrastructure.

Community Cloud

Hospital example: Several hospitals may share infrastructure designed around common privacy, security and compliance needs.

Hybrid Cloud

Locker + rented hall analogy: Keep sensitive items in your private locker, but use a public hall when you need extra capacity.
Deployment trick:
Private = Mine
Public = Everyone
Community = Similar Group
Hybrid = Mix

Imagine FoodFast buys and manages everything itself.

Networking
Storage
Cooling
Electricity backup

💸 High Cost

Rent, power, cooling and maintenance.

⏳ Slow Expansion

Adding or replacing hardware takes time.

📉 Limited Scaling

Capacity depends on what you physically own.

👨‍🔧 Monitoring Team

You need staff to manage infrastructure.

🔥 Disaster Risk

Fire, power shutdown or other disasters may affect systems.

How cloud helps: lower IT costs, better agility and easier scaling.

1. Highly Scalable & Flexible

2. Reduced Infrastructure Cost

Use provider infrastructure instead of buying and operating everything yourself.

3. Higher Security

24×7 Visibility

Continuous monitoring.

DDoS Protection

Protection mechanisms against distributed attacks.

Threat Detection

Advanced monitoring can identify suspicious activity.

4. High Availability

Traffic-police analogy: A load balancer distributes requests among multiple servers. If one fails, others can continue.

5. Disaster Recovery

Cloud backups can help restore data and services after emergencies or power outages.

6. Fault Tolerance

Chapter 5

⚠️ Disadvantages of Cloud Computing

🌐 Network Dependency

If connectivity fails, cloud access may be affected.

⛔ Downtime

Provider outages can make services temporarily unavailable.

🎛️ Limited Control

You may not control every part of the infrastructure.

🔐 Vulnerability to Attacks

Cloud services still face cybersecurity risks.

🔒 Vendor Lock-In

Moving from one provider to another can become difficult.

📊 Varied Performance / Support

Performance and support may differ.

Vendor lock-in analogy: Imagine a charger that works with only one brand. Once your system depends heavily on one provider, moving away may be difficult.
