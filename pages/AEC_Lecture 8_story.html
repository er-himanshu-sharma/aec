Chapter 1

🐦 Particle Swarm Optimization — Birds Searching for Food

PSO is a population-based stochastic optimization algorithm. Each particle is a candidate solution moving through a search space.

Bird story: Imagine 20 birds searching a large field for food. Each bird remembers the best place it has personally found, but the flock also shares information about the best place found by anyone. 
Bird = Particle
Food = Solution
pbest = My best location so far
gbest = Best location found by the whole flock

Current Position

Where the particle is now.

pbest

The best position this particle has found.

gbest

The best position discovered by the swarm.

Velocity and Position Update

V(t+1) = wV(t) + c1 × rand() × (Xpbest − X(t)) + c2 × rand() × (Xgbest − X(t))
X(t+1) = X(t) + V(t+1)
One-line meaning: Keep some of the old direction + move toward my best + move toward the group's best.

PSO Algorithm

1. Start with random particles and velocities
2. Evaluate fitness
3. Update pbest
4. Update gbest
5. Update velocity
6. Update position
7. Repeat until stopping condition
8. Return the best approximate solution
Chapter 2

🧮 One Discrete PSO Iteration for Cloud Scheduling

Cloud story: Suppose every task is a customer order and VM1, VM2 and VM3 are three kitchens. A particle is simply one possible assignment of tasks to kitchens. 
Current assignment:
xᵏ = [2,2,1,3,2,1]

Meaning:
T1 → VM2
T2 → VM2
T3 → VM1
T4 → VM3
T5 → VM2
T6 → VM1

The lecture gives:

pbest = [3,2,3,2,3,1]
gbest = [3,3,3,2,3,1]

Discrete PSO Idea

Instead of moving through continuous coordinates, each task has a score for VM1, VM2 and VM3. After updating the velocity scores, the VM with the largest score is selected.

New score = inertia part + personal-best influence + global-best influence

The lecture uses:

w = 0.7
c1 = c2 = 1.8
r1 = 0.3
r2 = 0.6

c1r1 = 0.54
c2r2 = 1.08

T1 Example

T1 currently sits on VM2, while both pbest and gbest say VM3.

Updated score vector for T1 = [0.14, 0.07, 1.98]
Largest component = VM3 → T1 moves VM2 → VM3.

T3 Example

T3 currently sits on VM1, while pbest and gbest again point to VM3.

Updated score vector for T3 = [0.28, 0.07, 2.12]
Largest component = VM3 → T3 moves VM1 → VM3.
Load-balancing lesson: PSO may move several tasks toward the same promising VM. That can improve fitness if VM3 is fast, but it may also increase concentration of load there.
Chapter 3

🧬 Genetic Algorithms — Nature Becomes an Optimization Teacher

Jungle story: Imagine a jungle with many animals. Some are better adapted than others. Food and water are limited, so the stronger or fitter individuals have a better chance to survive and reproduce. Their useful characteristics pass to the next generation.
Natural-selection rule: “Select the best, discard the rest.”

Genetic Algorithms use the same idea for optimization. Instead of animals, we have candidate solutions called chromosomes. 

Animal population → Population of candidate solutions
Fitness of animal → Fitness of solution
Mating → Crossover
Random genetic change → Mutation
Best adapted animal → Best solution

GA Working Cycle

1. Initialize Population
2. Evaluate Fitness
3. Selection
4. Crossover
5. Mutation
After mutation, evaluate the new population again. If the solution is good enough, stop. Otherwise repeat for another generation.
Chapter 4

🎯 Fitness Function — “How Good Is This Candidate?”

A fitness function gives a score to every chromosome so different solutions can be ranked.

Exam analogy: Think of fitness like marks in an exam. A student with better marks ranks higher. A chromosome with better fitness has a better chance to survive and reproduce.

The lecture gives TSP as an example: if the goal is a shorter route, the total route distance acts as the evaluation measure, and lower distance means a fitter solution.

Good fitness function: should reflect the real optimization goal and should be quick to compute.
Chapter 5

🎡 Selection — Who Gets to Become a Parent?

The lecture lists two common approaches:

Roulette Wheel Selection

Higher fitness gives a larger probability of being selected.

Tournament Selection

Candidate solutions compete and better ones are chosen.

Roulette Wheel Story

Imagine a wheel divided into slices. Better chromosomes get bigger slices. When the wheel spins, a bigger slice has a better chance of being selected.

Chapter 6

✂️ Crossover — Children Inherit From Two Parents

Family story: A child may inherit some traits from one parent and some from the other. In GA, two chromosomes exchange genetic material to create offspring.

Single-Point Crossover

Parent 1: 11011 | 00100110110
Parent 2: 11011 | 11000011110

Offspring 1: 11011 | 11000011110
Offspring 2: 11011 | 00100110110
Memory: Cut both parents at one position, then swap the pieces after the cut.
Chapter 7

👑 Elitism — Protect the Champion

Imagine the best player in your cricket team. You would not want to accidentally remove that player while forming the next team. Elitism directly copies some of the best chromosomes into the next generation.

This prevents a very good solution from being lost during crossover or mutation.

Elitism = Keep the best solution safe.
Chapter 8

🧪 Mutation — Small Random Change to Maintain Diversity

If every child were almost identical, the population could get stuck. Mutation introduces small random changes so the search can explore new possibilities. 
Original offspring:
11011 00100 110110

After mutation:
11010 00100 100110

For binary encoding, mutation can simply flip selected bits from 0 to 1 or 1 to 0.

Mutation probability controls how often parts of a chromosome are changed.
