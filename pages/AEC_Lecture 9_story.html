Chapter 7

🐜 Ant Colony Optimization — How Blind Ants Discover a Short Route

The second half of the lecture introduces Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), a population-based metaheuristic used to find approximate solutions to optimization problems represented as paths in weighted graphs.

Ant story: Ants leave their nest looking for food. They initially move almost randomly. As they walk, they leave a chemical trail called pheromone.

What Happens Next?

Ant explores path→ Finds food→ Leaves pheromone→ Other ants prefer stronger trail

The lecture explains that pheromone evaporates with time. On a shorter path, ants complete trips more quickly, so that path gets reinforced more often.

Key ACO idea: Short route → more frequent pheromone reinforcement → more ants choose it → route becomes even stronger.
In the lecture's visual sequence, ants initially explore multiple branches. Over time the shorter route gains stronger pheromone, while less useful routes fade.
Chapter 8

🧭 The ACO Algorithm in Simple Words

1. Construct Ant Solutions

Each ant extends a partial solution by probabilistically choosing edges using pheromone and problem information.

2. Local Search

An optional problem-specific improvement step.

3. Update Pheromones

Increase pheromone for good solutions and reduce it through evaporation.

Initialize pheromone trails
        ↓
While stopping condition not met:
    Construct ant solutions
    Apply local search (optional)
    Update pheromones
        ↓
Return best path / solution

Applications Listed in the Lecture

Shortest Path & Routing

Choose efficient routes in graphs or networks.

Assignment Problems

Assign jobs/resources using a search process.

Search combinational solution spaces.
