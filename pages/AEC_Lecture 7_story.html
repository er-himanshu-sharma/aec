📘 Lecture 7 • Story-Based Notes

Normalization, Fitness, Min-Min & Max-Min explained like a simple story

Imagine three cloud schedules are competing in a championship. One is faster, one is cheaper, one uses less energy. Lecture 7 teaches us how to put these different measurements on the same scale, combine them into one fitness score, and then build schedules using Min-Min and Max-Min heuristics.

The Lecture Journey

1. Normalize M, C and EConvert unlike values to a common 0–1 scale.
2. Evaluate candidate schedule SDCompute makespan, cost, energy and weighted fitness.
3. Min-MinPick the task with the smallest minimum completion time.
4. Max-MinPick the task with the largest minimum completion time.
Chapter 1

🏁 Why Do We Need Normalization?

Suppose three students are competing, but one score is in seconds, another in rupees and another in joules. We cannot directly add 15 seconds + ₹12 + 1804 joules.

Story: A judge first converts every category into a common 0-to-1 scale. In this lecture, the three categories are Makespan (M), Cost (C) and Energy (E).

x̂ = (x − xmin) / (xmax − xmin)
Meaning: Best/minimum value becomes close to 0, worst/maximum value becomes close to 1. Since M, C and E are all things we want to minimize, a smaller normalized value is better.

Chapter 3

⚖️ Normalize SD and Compute Fitness

Judge story: Now the judge converts all three scores of SD onto the same 0–1 scale and then gives them different importance weights.

Normalized Makespan

M̂ = (23.8 − 15.1) / (35.6 − 15.1) ≈ 0.4243902439

Normalized Cost

Ĉ = (11.7667578125 − 11.5067578125) / (12.0267578125 − 11.5067578125) = 0.5

Normalized Energy

Ê = (1945.33 − 1804) / (2133.34 − 1804) ≈ 0.4291497976

Weighted Fitness

Fitness D = 0.5M̂ + 0.3Ĉ + 0.2Ê
≈ 0.5(0.4243902439) + 0.3(0.5) + 0.2(0.4291497976)
≈ 0.4480250815
Memory: 50% importance to makespan, 30% to cost and 20% to energy. Because these are minimization objectives, a lower fitness score is preferable under this formula.
Chapter 4

🐭 Min-Min Heuristic — “Finish the Easiest Winner First”

Min-Min repeatedly finds the earliest possible finish time for every remaining task, then chooses the task whose best finish time is the smallest of all.

Restaurant story: Six orders are waiting. For each order, ask “Which chef can finish this order earliest?” Then among those six best options, pick the order that can finish earliest overall.
T6 → VM3 because the smallest minimum is 0.6.
T2 → VM2 because after VM3 becomes busy, T2 can finish in 1.34 on VM2.
T4 → VM3 with minimum completion time 1.8.
T1 → VM1 with minimum 3.5.
T5 → VM3 with minimum 4.2.
T3 → VM2 as the last remaining task.
Final Min-Min assignment order
T6 → T2 → T4 → T1 → T5 → T3
VM sequence: [3, 2, 3, 1, 3, 2]
One-line memory: Min-Min = among all tasks, choose the smallest of their minimum completion times.
Chapter 5

🐘 Max-Min Heuristic — “Give the Biggest Best-Case Job Priority”

Max-Min also finds the minimum completion time for each task, but then selects the task whose minimum is the largest.

Restaurant story: First find each order’s fastest chef. Then ask, “Which order is still the slowest even in its best case?” Serve that bigger/longer order first so it does not wait too long.
T3 → VM3 because the maximum among the task minimums is 2.4.
T5 → VM2 with current selected minimum 3.0.
T1 → VM1 with 3.5.
T4 → VM3 with 3.6.
T2 → VM2 with 4.34.
T6 → VM3 as the final task.
Final Max-Min assignment order
T3 → T5 → T1 → T4 → T2 → T6
VM sequence: [3, 2, 1, 3, 2, 3]
One-line memory: Max-Min = among all tasks, choose the largest of their minimum completion times.
